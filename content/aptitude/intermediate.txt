2. NUMBER SYSTEM

The number system is used to represent and classify numbers.

Important concepts:

• Divisibility
• Factors
• Multiples
• Prime numbers
• HCF and LCM
• Remainders
• Unit digits
• Number patterns


3. DIVISIBILITY RULE FOR 4

A number is divisible by 4 if its last two digits are divisible
by 4.

Example:

312

Last two digits = 12

12 is divisible by 4.

Therefore, 312 is divisible by 4.


4. DIVISIBILITY RULE FOR 8

A number is divisible by 8 if its last three digits are divisible
by 8.

Example:

1,216

Last three digits = 216

216 ÷ 8 = 27

Therefore, 1216 is divisible by 8.


5. DIVISIBILITY RULE FOR 9

A number is divisible by 9 if the sum of its digits is divisible
by 9.

Example:

729

7 + 2 + 9 = 18

18 is divisible by 9.

Therefore, 729 is divisible by 9.


6. DIVISIBILITY RULE FOR 11

For divisibility by 11, find the difference between the sums of
alternate digits.

Example:

121

(1 + 1) - 2 = 0

Therefore, 121 is divisible by 11.


7. REMAINDER

The remainder is the value left after division.

Example:

17 ÷ 5

Quotient = 3
Remainder = 2


8. PERCENTAGE CHANGE

Percentage change:

(Change / Original Value) × 100

If a value increases from 200 to 250:

Change = 50

Percentage increase:

(50 / 200) × 100 = 25%


9. SUCCESSIVE PERCENTAGE CHANGE

If a value changes by x% and then by y%, the overall percentage
change is:

x + y + (xy / 100)

The signs of x and y should be considered according to whether the
change is an increase or decrease.


10. PERCENTAGE AND POPULATION

Percentage calculations are commonly used for:

• Population growth
• Salary increase
• Price changes
• Marks
• Production


11. PROFIT AND LOSS

Important formulas:

Profit = SP - CP

Loss = CP - SP

Profit% = (Profit / CP) × 100

Loss% = (Loss / CP) × 100


12. DISCOUNT

Discount is the reduction given on the marked price.

Discount:

Marked Price - Selling Price

Discount percentage:

(Discount / Marked Price) × 100


13. SELLING PRICE AFTER DISCOUNT

Selling Price:

Marked Price × (100 - Discount%) / 100


14. SUCCESSIVE DISCOUNTS

When two discounts are applied successively:

Equivalent discount:

a + b - (ab / 100)

where a and b are the two discount percentages.


15. RATIO

A ratio compares two quantities of the same type.

Example:

20 : 30

Divide by 10:

2 : 3


16. PROPORTION

If:

a : b = c : d

then:

a/b = c/d

and:

ad = bc


17. DIRECT PROPORTION

If two quantities increase or decrease together in the same ratio,
they are directly proportional.

Example:

If 1 notebook costs Rs. 20,

5 notebooks cost:

5 × 20 = Rs. 100


18. INVERSE PROPORTION

If one quantity increases while the other decreases in such a way
that their product remains constant, they are inversely
proportional.

Example:

More workers can complete the same work in fewer days, assuming
equal efficiency.


19. PARTNERSHIP

Partnership problems involve sharing profit among partners.

Profit is generally divided according to:

Capital × Time


20. PARTNERSHIP EXAMPLE

A invests Rs. 10,000 for 12 months.

B invests Rs. 20,000 for 6 months.

A's ratio:

10,000 × 12 = 120,000

B's ratio:

20,000 × 6 = 120,000

Therefore:

A : B = 1 : 1


21. AVERAGE

Average:

Sum of observations / Number of observations


22. AVERAGE WITH A NEW VALUE

If the average of n values is A, then:

Total = n × A

When a new value is added:

New Average = New Total / New Number of Values


23. WEIGHTED AVERAGE

Weighted average gives different importance to different values.

Formula:

Weighted Average =
Σ(value × weight) / Σweights


24. SIMPLE INTEREST

Simple Interest:

SI = (P × R × T) / 100

Amount:

A = P + SI


25. COMPOUND INTEREST

Compound Interest is calculated on the principal plus accumulated
interest.

For annual compounding:

A = P(1 + R/100)^T

Compound Interest:

CI = A - P


26. SIMPLE VS COMPOUND INTEREST

Simple Interest:

Interest is calculated only on the original principal.

Compound Interest:

Interest is calculated on the accumulated amount.


27. COMPOUND INTEREST EXAMPLE

Suppose:

P = Rs. 1000
R = 10%
T = 2 years

Amount:

A = 1000(1.10)^2

A = Rs. 1210

Compound Interest:

1210 - 1000 = Rs. 210


28. TIME AND WORK

If A completes a work in x days:

A's one-day work = 1/x


29. WORKING TOGETHER

If A completes work in 10 days and B in 15 days:

A's work per day = 1/10

B's work per day = 1/15

Together:

1/10 + 1/15

= 1/6

Therefore, they complete the work in 6 days.


30. EFFICIENCY

Efficiency is inversely related to the time required for the same
amount of work.

If A is twice as efficient as B:

A takes half the time taken by B.


31. PIPES AND CISTERNS

Pipes and cistern problems are similar to time and work problems.

A pipe that fills a tank in 10 hours fills:

1/10 of the tank per hour.


32. EMPTYING PIPE

If a pipe empties a tank in 20 hours, its work rate is:

-1/20

The negative sign represents removal of water.


33. COMBINED PIPES

If one pipe fills a tank in 10 hours and another empties it in
20 hours:

Net work per hour:

1/10 - 1/20

= 1/20

Therefore, the tank fills in 20 hours.


34. TIME, SPEED AND DISTANCE

Basic formulas:

Distance = Speed × Time

Speed = Distance / Time

Time = Distance / Speed


35. AVERAGE SPEED

Average speed is:

Total Distance / Total Time

It is not always equal to the simple average of speeds.


36. RELATIVE SPEED

Relative speed depends on the direction of motion.

For objects moving in the same direction:

Relative Speed = Difference of speeds

For objects moving in opposite directions:

Relative Speed = Sum of speeds


37. TRAIN PROBLEMS

When a train passes a stationary object:

Distance covered is generally the length of the train.

Time:

Distance / Speed


38. TRAIN CROSSING A PLATFORM

When a train crosses a platform:

Distance covered:

Length of train + Length of platform

Time:

Total distance / Speed


39. UNIT CONVERSION FOR SPEED

Important conversion:

1 km/h = 5/18 m/s

1 m/s = 18/5 km/h


40. BOATS AND STREAMS

Let:

Speed of boat in still water = B

Speed of stream = S


41. DOWNSTREAM SPEED

Downstream speed:

B + S


42. UPSTREAM SPEED

Upstream speed:

B - S


43. BOAT EXAMPLE

If boat speed is 10 km/h and stream speed is 2 km/h:

Downstream:

10 + 2 = 12 km/h

Upstream:

10 - 2 = 8 km/h


44. PROBABILITY

Probability:

Favourable outcomes / Total possible outcomes


45. COMPLEMENTARY PROBABILITY

Probability of an event not occurring:

P(not A) = 1 - P(A)


46. INDEPENDENT EVENTS

Two events are independent if the occurrence of one does not affect
the probability of the other.

For independent events:

P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B)


47. ADDITION RULE OF PROBABILITY

For mutually exclusive events:

P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B)


48. PERMUTATION

Permutation deals with arrangements where order matters.

Formula:

nPr = n! / (n-r)!


49. COMBINATION

Combination deals with selections where order does not matter.

Formula:

nCr = n! / [r!(n-r)!]


50. FACTORIAL

Factorial of n:

n! = n × (n-1) × (n-2) × ... × 1

Example:

5! = 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1

= 120


51. PERMUTATION EXAMPLE

Number of ways to arrange 3 different objects:

3!

= 3 × 2 × 1

= 6


52. COMBINATION EXAMPLE

Number of ways to select 2 objects from 4:

4C2

= 4! / (2! × 2!)

= 6


53. ALGEBRAIC IDENTITIES

Important identities:

(a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²

(a - b)² = a² - 2ab + b²

(a + b)(a - b) = a² - b²


54. LINEAR EQUATIONS

Example:

3x + 5 = 20

3x = 15

x = 5


55. SIMULTANEOUS EQUATIONS

Two equations can be solved together.

Example:

x + y = 10

x - y = 2

Adding:

2x = 12

x = 6

Therefore:

y = 4


56. QUADRATIC EQUATION

A quadratic equation has the form:

ax² + bx + c = 0

The quadratic formula is:

x = [-b ± √(b² - 4ac)] / 2a

58. TRIANGLE

Sum of interior angles of a triangle:

180°


59. PYTHAGOREAN THEOREM

For a right-angled triangle:

a² + b² = c²

where c is the hypotenuse.


60. AREA OF TRIANGLE

Area:

1/2 × Base × Height


61. AREA OF CIRCLE

Area:

πr²


62. CIRCUMFERENCE

Circumference:

2πr

64. DATA INTERPRETATION STRATEGY

Follow these steps:

1. Read the title.
2. Understand the units.
3. Identify the required values.
4. Compare relevant data.
5. Perform calculations.
6. Check the answer.


65. RATIO IN DATA INTERPRETATION

If a table shows:

A = 200

B = 300

Ratio:

A : B = 200 : 300

= 2 : 3


66. PERCENTAGE IN DATA INTERPRETATION

Percentage contribution:

Value / Total × 100

73. SYLLOGISM

A syllogism contains statements and conclusions.

Example:

All cats are animals.

Tom is a cat.

Therefore:

Tom is an animal.


74. STATEMENT AND CONCLUSION

A conclusion should be selected only if it logically follows from
the given statement.

Do not use outside assumptions.


75. CLOCK PROBLEMS

The clock has:

• 12 hours
• 60 minutes
• 360 degrees

Therefore:

1 hour = 30°

1 minute = 6°


76. CLOCK ANGLE

The minute hand moves faster than the hour hand.

Basic formula for the angle between hands:

|30H - 5.5M|

where:

H = hour
M = minutes


77. CALENDAR

Important facts:

Normal year = 365 days

Leap year = 366 days

Normal year has:

52 weeks + 1 day

Leap year has:

52 weeks + 2 days


78. LEAP YEAR

A year is generally a leap year if:

• It is divisible by 4.

For century years, it must also be divisible by 400.

Example:

2000 is a leap year.

1900 is not a leap year.

80. AGE EXAMPLE

If A is 5 years older than B and B is 20:

A = 20 + 5

A = 25


81. MIXTURES

Mixture problems involve combining quantities with different

82. ALLIGATION

Alligation is a shortcut method for solving certain mixture
problems.

It compares:

Lower value
Mean value
Higher value


83. PARTNERSHIP AND PROFIT

Profit sharing is proportional to:

Investment × Time

If two partners invest different amounts for different periods,
calculate their capital-time products before dividing profit.


84. BASIC SET THEORY

For two sets A and B:

Union:

A ∪ B

Intersection:

A ∩ B


85. VENN DIAGRAM

Venn diagrams can be used to solve:

• Set problems
• Survey problems
• Grouping problems
• Common and unique elements


86. SET FORMULA

For two sets:

n(A ∪ B) = n(A) + n(B) - n(A ∩ B)


87. WORKING WITH FRACTIONS

For addition:

a/b + c/d = (ad + bc)/bd

For subtraction:

a/b - c/d = (ad - bc)/bd


88. RATIO AND FRACTION

A ratio of:

2 : 3

means:

2/3

when comparing the first quantity with the second.

91. APPROXIMATION

Approximation is useful when exact calculation is unnecessary.

Round numbers to convenient values and estimate the answer.


92. INTERMEDIATE PROBLEM-SOLVING STRATEGY

For every question:

1. Read carefully.
2. Identify the topic.
3. Write the known values.
4. Select the formula.
5. Solve step by step.
6. Check units.
7. Check whether the answer is reasonable.


93. TIME MANAGEMENT

For placement aptitude tests:

• Start with easy questions.
• Avoid spending too long on one problem.
• Use shortcuts when appropriate.
• Skip difficult questions temporarily.
• Return to skipped questions later.
• Keep a few minutes for checking.


94. COMMON MISTAKES

Avoid:

• Using the wrong formula.
• Confusing CP and SP.
• Confusing percentage with percentage points.
• Forgetting unit conversion.
• Using average speed incorrectly.
• Ignoring negative signs.
• Making assumptions in reasoning questions.

   5C2

8. Find:

   5P2

9. Solve:

   x + y = 12
   x - y = 4

    2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ?

SUMMARY

Intermediate aptitude develops the ability to solve multi-step
problems quickly and accurately.

The learner should be comfortable with mathematical formulas,
percentages, ratios, averages, interest, work, speed, probability,
permutation, combination, algebra, geometry and reasoning.

Regular timed practice is important for placement and competitive
examinations.
