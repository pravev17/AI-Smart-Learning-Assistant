APTITUDE – BEGINNER LEVEL
=========================

1. INTRODUCTION TO APTITUDE

Aptitude is the ability to solve problems using logical thinking,
basic mathematics and reasoning.

Aptitude is commonly used in:

• Placement tests
• Competitive examinations
• Entrance examinations
• Job assessments
• Interview screening tests

Important beginner topics include:

• Number system
• Simplification
• HCF and LCM
• Percentages
• Profit and loss
• Ratio and proportion
• Average
• Simple interest
• Time and work
• Time, speed and distance
• Basic probability
• Basic data interpretation
• Logical reasoning


2. NUMBER SYSTEM

A number system is a method of representing numbers.

Common types of numbers include:

• Natural numbers
• Whole numbers
• Integers
• Rational numbers
• Irrational numbers
• Real numbers


3. NATURAL NUMBERS

Natural numbers are positive counting numbers.

Examples:

1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, ...

Natural numbers are commonly used for counting.


4. WHOLE NUMBERS

Whole numbers include zero and all positive integers.

Examples:

0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, ...


5. INTEGERS

Integers include positive numbers, negative numbers and zero.

Examples:

..., -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ...


6. EVEN NUMBERS

An even number is divisible by 2.

Examples:

2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12


7. ODD NUMBERS

An odd number is not divisible by 2.

Examples:

1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11


8. PRIME NUMBERS

A prime number has exactly two positive factors:

1 and the number itself.

Examples:

2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17


9. COMPOSITE NUMBERS

A composite number has more than two positive factors.

Examples:

4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12


10. FACTORS

A factor is a number that divides another number exactly.

Example:

Factors of 12 are:

1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 12.


11. MULTIPLES

Multiples are obtained by multiplying a number by whole numbers.

Example:

Multiples of 5:

5, 10, 15, 20, 25, ...


12. DIVISIBILITY RULES

Divisibility rules help determine whether a number can be divided
by another number without a remainder.

Divisible by 2:

The last digit is even.

Example:

246 is divisible by 2.


Divisible by 5:

The last digit is 0 or 5.

Example:

125 is divisible by 5.


Divisible by 10:

The last digit is 0.

Example:

340 is divisible by 10.


Divisible by 3:

The sum of digits is divisible by 3.

Example:

123

1 + 2 + 3 = 6

Therefore, 123 is divisible by 3.


13. HCF

HCF stands for Highest Common Factor.

It is the greatest number that divides two or more numbers exactly.

Example:

HCF of 12 and 18 is 6.


14. LCM

LCM stands for Least Common Multiple.

It is the smallest positive number that is a multiple of two or
more numbers.

Example:

LCM of 4 and 6 is 12.


15. HCF AND LCM RELATIONSHIP

For two positive integers:

Product of numbers = HCF × LCM

Example:

Numbers = 4 and 6

HCF = 2

LCM = 12

2 × 12 = 24

4 × 6 = 24


16. SIMPLIFICATION

Simplification means solving mathematical expressions by following
the correct order of operations.

A common order is:

B – Brackets
O – Orders
D – Division
M – Multiplication
A – Addition
S – Subtraction

Example:

10 + 5 × 2

First multiply:

5 × 2 = 10

Then add:

10 + 10 = 20


17. FRACTIONS

A fraction represents a part of a whole.

Example:

3/4

Here:

3 = numerator
4 = denominator


18. TYPES OF FRACTIONS

Proper fraction:

Numerator is smaller than denominator.

Example:

3/5


Improper fraction:

Numerator is greater than or equal to denominator.

Example:

7/5


Mixed fraction:

A whole number and a fraction are combined.

Example:

1 2/3


19. DECIMAL NUMBERS

A decimal represents a number using a decimal point.

Examples:

0.5
2.75
10.25


20. CONVERSION OF FRACTION TO DECIMAL

Divide the numerator by the denominator.

Example:

1/2 = 0.5

3/4 = 0.75


21. PERCENTAGE

Percentage means a value out of 100.

Symbol:

%

Example:

25% means 25 out of 100.


22. PERCENTAGE FORMULA

Percentage:

(Part / Whole) × 100


23. PERCENTAGE EXAMPLE

If a student scores 40 marks out of 50:

Percentage:

(40 / 50) × 100

= 80%

Therefore, the student scored 80%.


24. CONVERTING PERCENTAGE TO DECIMAL

Divide the percentage by 100.

Example:

25% = 25/100 = 0.25


25. PERCENTAGE INCREASE

Percentage increase:

(Increase / Original Value) × 100


26. PERCENTAGE DECREASE

Percentage decrease:

(Decrease / Original Value) × 100


27. PROFIT

Profit occurs when the selling price is greater than the cost price.

Profit:

Selling Price - Cost Price


28. LOSS

Loss occurs when the selling price is less than the cost price.

Loss:

Cost Price - Selling Price


29. PROFIT PERCENTAGE

Profit percentage:

(Profit / Cost Price) × 100


30. LOSS PERCENTAGE

Loss percentage:

(Loss / Cost Price) × 100


31. COST PRICE

Cost Price (CP) is the price paid to purchase an item.


32. SELLING PRICE

Selling Price (SP) is the price at which an item is sold.


33. RATIO

A ratio compares two quantities.

Example:

If there are 2 boys and 3 girls:

Ratio of boys to girls:

2 : 3


34. RATIO SIMPLIFICATION

Divide both terms by their common factor.

Example:

10 : 15

Divide by 5:

2 : 3


35. PROPORTION

A proportion states that two ratios are equal.

Example:

2 : 4 = 3 : 6

Both ratios represent the same relationship.


36. DIRECT PROPORTION

In direct proportion, when one quantity increases, the other
quantity also increases in the same proportion.

Example:

More items generally require more total cost when price per item
is fixed.


37. INVERSE PROPORTION

In inverse proportion, when one quantity increases, the other
quantity decreases.

Example:

For a fixed amount of work, more workers may require fewer days.


38. AVERAGE

Average is calculated by:

Average = Sum of values / Number of values


39. AVERAGE EXAMPLE

Find the average of:

10, 20 and 30

Sum:

10 + 20 + 30 = 60

Number of values = 3

Average:

60 / 3 = 20


40. SIMPLE INTEREST

Simple Interest is interest calculated only on the original
principal amount.

Formula:

SI = (P × R × T) / 100

Where:

P = Principal
R = Rate of interest
T = Time


41. SIMPLE INTEREST EXAMPLE

If:

P = 1000
R = 10%
T = 2 years

SI:

(1000 × 10 × 2) / 100

= 200


42. AMOUNT

Amount is the total money after adding interest to the principal.

Amount:

Principal + Simple Interest


43. TIME AND WORK

Time and Work problems deal with the amount of work completed by
people or machines.

If a person completes a work in 10 days:

Work completed in one day:

1/10


44. WORK TOGETHER

If A completes a work in 10 days and B completes it in 20 days:

A's one-day work = 1/10

B's one-day work = 1/20

Together:

1/10 + 1/20

= 3/20


45. TIME, SPEED AND DISTANCE

The basic relationship is:

Distance = Speed × Time


46. SPEED

Speed is the distance travelled per unit of time.

Formula:

Speed = Distance / Time


47. TIME

Time can be calculated using:

Time = Distance / Speed


48. DISTANCE

Distance can be calculated using:

Distance = Speed × Time


49. SPEED EXAMPLE

If a vehicle travels 120 km in 3 hours:

Speed:

120 / 3

= 40 km/h


50. UNIT CONVERSION

Common conversions:

1 kilometre = 1000 metres

1 metre = 100 centimetres

1 hour = 60 minutes

1 minute = 60 seconds


51. BASIC PROBABILITY

Probability measures the chance of an event occurring.

Formula:

Probability = Favourable Outcomes / Total Outcomes


52. PROBABILITY EXAMPLE

A coin has two possible outcomes:

Head
Tail

Probability of getting Head:

1/2


53. BASIC DATA INTERPRETATION

Data Interpretation involves understanding information presented
in:

• Tables
• Bar charts
• Line charts
• Pie charts
• Graphs


54. TABLES

A table organizes information into rows and columns.

Example:

Month       Sales

January     100
February    120
March       150


55. BAR CHART

A bar chart represents data using rectangular bars.

The length or height of each bar represents the value.


56. PIE CHART

A pie chart represents data as parts of a whole.

The complete circle represents:

100%


57. BASIC LOGICAL REASONING

Logical reasoning tests the ability to identify patterns,
relationships and conclusions.


58. NUMBER SERIES

A number series contains numbers following a particular pattern.

Example:

2, 4, 6, 8, 10

The pattern is:

+2


59. ANOTHER NUMBER SERIES

Example:

5, 10, 15, 20, 25

The pattern is:

+5


60. ALPHABET SERIES

Alphabet series questions involve patterns using letters.

Example:

A, C, E, G, I

The pattern skips one letter each time.


61. ODD ONE OUT

In an odd-one-out question, identify the item that does not belong
to the same group.

Example:

Apple, Mango, Banana, Carrot

Carrot is different because it is a vegetable.


62. CODING AND DECODING

Coding-decoding questions replace letters or words according to
a particular rule.

Example:

If A = 1, B = 2, C = 3,

then:

CAB = 3, 1, 2


63. ANALOGY

Analogy questions identify a relationship between two pairs.

Example:

Bird : Fly :: Fish : Swim


64. DIRECTIONS

Direction questions involve:

• North
• South
• East
• West


65. BASIC DIRECTION EXAMPLE

If a person moves:

5 km North

and then

5 km South

the person returns to the starting point.


66. BLOOD RELATIONSHIPS

Blood relation questions identify relationships between family
members.

Common relationships:

• Father
• Mother
• Brother
• Sister
• Son
• Daughter
• Grandfather
• Grandmother


67. CLOCK BASICS

A clock has:

12 numbers
60 minutes
60 seconds in one minute

The minute hand completes one full rotation in 60 minutes.


68. CALENDAR BASICS

A normal year has:

365 days

A leap year has:

366 days

A week has:

7 days


69. BASIC AGES

Age problems involve the present, past or future ages of people.

Example:

If a person is 20 years old now, after 5 years the person will be:

20 + 5 = 25 years old.


70. BASIC MIXTURES

Mixture problems involve combining two or more quantities.

Examples:

• Water and milk
• Different prices of materials
• Different concentrations


71. BASIC PERMUTATION

Permutation deals with arrangements where order matters.

Example:

Arrangements of A and B:

AB
BA

There are 2 arrangements.


72. BASIC COMBINATION

Combination deals with selecting items where order does not matter.

Example:

Selecting 2 students from a group is a combination problem when
the order of selection is not important.


73. BASIC SETS

A set is a collection of distinct objects.

Example:

A = {1, 2, 3, 4}


74. VENN DIAGRAM

A Venn diagram represents relationships between sets using circles.

It can be used to find:

• Common elements
• Different elements
• Union
• Intersection


75. BASIC ALGEBRA

Algebra uses letters to represent unknown values.

Example:

x + 5 = 10

Therefore:

x = 5


76. LINEAR EQUATION

A linear equation has variables with power 1.

Example:

2x + 4 = 10

2x = 6

x = 3


77. BASIC GEOMETRY

Important basic shapes include:

• Square
• Rectangle
• Triangle
• Circle


78. RECTANGLE

Area:

Length × Breadth

Perimeter:

2 × (Length + Breadth)


79. SQUARE

Area:

Side × Side

Perimeter:

4 × Side


80. TRIANGLE

Area:

1/2 × Base × Height


81. CIRCLE

Area:

πr²

Circumference:

2πr

where:

r = radius


82. BASIC DATA COMPARISON

When comparing values, check:

• Greater value
• Smaller value
• Difference
• Percentage change
• Ratio


83. APPROXIMATION

Approximation means finding a value that is close to the exact
answer.

It is useful for quickly solving aptitude questions.


84. SIMPLIFICATION TIPS

For faster calculations:

• Memorize multiplication tables.
• Learn common fraction-to-percentage conversions.
• Practice squares and cubes.
• Use divisibility rules.
• Follow the correct order of operations.

88. APTITUDE PROBLEM-SOLVING METHOD

Follow these steps:

1. Read the question carefully.
2. Identify the given values.
3. Identify what needs to be found.
4. Select the appropriate formula.
5. Substitute the values.
6. Calculate carefully.
7. Check the answer.


89. TIME MANAGEMENT

In aptitude tests, time management is important.

Useful strategy:

• Solve easy questions first.
• Do not spend too much time on one question.
• Mark difficult questions for later.
• Check calculations when time is available.


90. COMMON APTITUDE MISTAKES

Avoid:

• Reading the question incorrectly.
• Using the wrong formula.
• Forgetting units.
• Making calculation errors.
• Spending too much time on one question.
• Ignoring negative values.
• Not checking the final answer.

SUMMARY

Beginner aptitude focuses on building strong mathematical and
logical reasoning fundamentals.

The most important areas are numbers, percentages, ratios,
averages, profit and loss, simple interest, time and work,
speed-distance-time, probability and basic reasoning.

Regular practice improves calculation speed, accuracy and
problem-solving ability.
